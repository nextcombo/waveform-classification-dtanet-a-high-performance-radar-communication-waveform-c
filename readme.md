# Waveform Classification: DTANet: A High-Performance Radar-Communication Waveform Classification Network Based on Decoupled 3D Attention Mechanism [with Python code]

Two. Summary This paper proposes DTANet, a dual-branch hybrid architecture that integrates Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) with Transformer to enhance waveform recognition performance in integrated radar-communication systems. The model generates high-resolution spectral plots through the smoothing of the Smoothed Pseudo-Wigner-Ville Distribution (SPWVD), and processes them using two parallel branches to capture fine-grained local details and model long-range dependencies respectively. As a core innovation, the Decoupled Attention (DTA) mechanism decomposes attention into three complementary paths: Wide-Time Attention (WTA) for capturing time-varying features, High-Frequency Attention (HFA) for learning frequency-aware interactions, and Channel Attention (CA) for recalibrating the importance of learned features, thereby constructing more robust feature representations while maintaining high computational efficiency. Experiments conducted on a dataset containing 12 synthetic waveforms demonstrate that DTANet achieves state-of-the-art performance with an accuracy of 91.11%, inference time of only 0.596 milliseconds, and a parameter size of only 75K. These results highlight the superiority of DTANet in terms of accuracy, speed, and compactness, making it a robust and efficient solution for real-time deployment on resource-constrained platforms.
Keywords: Deep Learning; Integrated Radar-Communication Systems; Waveform Classification

Three. Introduction With the rapid development of wireless technologies in smart cities, the electromagnetic spectrum is becoming increasingly congested. The overlap between radar and communication signals can reduce the performance of critical low-latency applications such as traffic management and collision avoidance [1]. Although these applications require accurate and real-time waveform recognition, distinguishing complex signal patterns in noisy and dynamic urban environments remains a significant challenge, which affects the reliability of integrated perception and communication systems [2^,3^]. This situation has driven the development of an effective method to improve waveform recognition technology used in environmental monitoring and vehicle navigation within integrated radar-communication (IRC) systems.
In the IRC environment, recognizing complex waveforms has become a significant research topic, prompting a shift in research direction from traditional machine learning to more adaptive deep learning frameworks. Deep models based on convolutional neural network (CNN) architecture have demonstrated strong capabilities for extracting original signal features, effectively addressing issues of noise and channel damage.
Early studies primarily employed CNNs for radar and communication waveform classification. Some models incorporated hybrid attention mechanisms and skip connections to enhance recognition performance[3], while others utilized multi-view time-frequency fusion to improve generalization capabilities[4]. Although these methods achieved certain effects, they also resulted in significant computational overhead. Subsequent research focused on developing lightweight models suitable for joint tasks (such as RACNN[5]), but these models often lacked robustness under complex noise conditions. A significant advancement was RadComNet[6], which balanced performance with complexity by using smooth pseudo-Wigner-Ville distribution (SPWVD) for time-frequency analysis (TFA) and integrating deep separable convolution. However, similar to many CNN-based models, this model still struggled to effectively capture the inherent long-range dependencies in complex signal structures.
To address this limitation, the Transformer-based architecture emerged. The Visual Transformer (ViT) [7] introduced an expandable self-attention mechanism to achieve global context modeling and achieved state-of-the-art performance in classification tasks. However, due to its higher computational and memory requirements, it is difficult to be deployed practically. Therefore, subsequent research focused on improving efficiency, including delayed-optimized Transformer variants [8], multi-scale attention mechanisms [9], and knowledge distillation techniques [10]. Although these methods reduced computational complexity, they often struggled to maintain high accuracy when processing complex or noisy waveform patterns. In recent years, the hybrid CNN-Transformer architecture has been proposed, aiming to bridge the gap between local modeling efficiency and global context perception. For example, the dual-branch architecture [11] achieved excellent classification accuracy under low signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) conditions, but at a significant computational cost. Thus, exploring and improving hybrid architectures that integrate the local efficiency of CNNs with the global modeling capability of Transformers has become an important direction for achieving efficient, accurate, and resource-aware radar-communication waveform recognition.
To address the above challenges, this paper proposes DTANet, a novel CNN-Transformer hybrid architecture specifically designed to enhance waveform recognition performance in IRC systems. By integrating the fine-grained local feature extraction capabilities of CNNs with the global context modeling ability of Transformers, DTANet achieves a better balance of accuracy and efficiency compared to existing models. The main contributions are as follows:
1.  
Proposes the DTANet dual-branch hybrid architecture, which processes SPWVD spectral graphs through two parallel branches: the CNN branch for local feature refinement, and the Transformer branch for global context modeling;
Introducing a novel decoupling attention (DTA) mechanism as the core of Transformer branches, which includes three parallel paths (width-direction temporal attention WTA, height-direction frequency attention HFA, channel attention CA), forcing the model to learn from different time, frequency and channel perspectives, achieving multidimensional feature representation with dimensionality reduction.
3  
Provides robust performance benchmarks for waveform recognition in IRC systems. On a dataset containing 12 classes of synthetic signals (including real channel impairments), DTANet achieved state-of-the-art results with an accuracy of 91.11% and a inference time of only 0.596 ms on this fundamental task.

SPWVD effectively suppresses crosstalk by applying smoothing operations in both the time and frequency domains, while avoiding the fixed resolution trade-off of Short-Time Fourier Transform (STFT) and the dependence on mother wavelet selection in Continuous Wavelet Transform (CWT), providing high time-frequency joint resolution and excellent energy concentration. (II) DTANet: A Mixed Architecture with Decoupled Attention
1  
Calibrated Residual Feature Extractor: The input spectrogram is preprocessed by a module that includes 7×7 convolution, batch normalization, ReLU activation, and max pooling to reduce spatial resolution and enhance computational efficiency. Subsequently, four SRes modules (fusing the ResNet residual mechanism with SE modules) are used to calibrate channel features. Finally, global average pooling (gap) aggregates spatial information into compact channel descriptors.
2
Decoupled Attention Transformer: This branch aims to capture long-range dependencies and global context. It generates a sequence of tokens containing both feature and position information by segmenting the spectrogram into non-overlapping patches, linearly projecting them to high-dimensional embedding spaces, and adding learnable positional embeddings. The input is fed into an Transformer encoder (TE) block.
•
TE Block: Employing a prelayer normalization architecture, the core innovation is replacing standard multi-head self-attention with DTA mechanism. The processing pipeline involves layer normalization for stabilizing training first, followed by DTA and MLP (including two fully connected layers with GELU activation), and finally output through residual connections.
•
DTA Mechanism: Dissecting attention into three independent parallel branches aligned with the IRC signal's physical structure: WTA branch models temporal dependencies along the time axis, capturing features like radar pulse sequentiality; HFA branch calculates attention along the frequency axis, identifying spectral characteristics of communication modulation; CA branch independently recalibrates the importance of token embeddings in the token embeddings, enhancing robustness against channel impairments. The outputs from these three branches are combined and linearly projected to obtain the final DTA output.
3
Feature Fusion and Classification: The output from the Transformer branch is averaged down to form a global feature vector. This is then concatenated with the local feature vector obtained from the calibrated residual feature extractor to form a unified feature vector that integrates local details with global spatiotemporal relationships. This vector is passed into a classification head comprising two fully connected layers and softmax for classification. The model is trained using the Adam optimizer for 60 rounds, with an initial learning rate of 0.001 (decayed by 5 times every 10 rounds), and a batch size of 32.
V. Conclusion This paper proposes DTANet, an efficient hybrid architecture, whose core is the novel decoupled attention (DTA) mechanism that enhances feature learning capabilities while reducing computational complexity. By decoupling attention into three paths: time, frequency, and channel, DTANet effectively captures both local and global features, thus outperforming existing state-of-the-art models such as RadComNet and VT-CMNet. The model achieves an accuracy of 91.11% and a inference time of 0.596 milliseconds, demonstrating excellent performance and efficiency balance. Despite its outstanding performance on comprehensive synthetic datasets, future research will focus on enhancing practical applicability by improving robustness under low SNR conditions through signal enhancement and DTA optimization; validating the model's generalization capability on real-world benchmark datasets; and expanding the classification scope to include modern 5G/6G waveforms such as OFDM and OTFS, along with higher-order modulation methods.
